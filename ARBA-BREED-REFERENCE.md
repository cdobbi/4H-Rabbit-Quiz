# Rabbit-Savvy ARBA Breed and Judging Reference

This document records the project owner's supplied breed specifications and terminology. Use it as the source of truth when updating the question bank.

## Class and Terminology

- Rex is a 4-class breed. Do not change it to 6-class.
- A class is a weight category: 4-class breeds have junior and senior divisions for each sex; 6-class breeds add intermediate divisions.
- A variety is a color division within a breed or group.
- A group is a broad color or pattern grouping.
- Type means body conformation.
- The five body types are Commercial, Compact, Cylindrical, Semi-arch, and Full-arch.
- Lionhead is Compact.

## Body Types and Classes

- Commercial: French Angora 4, American Sable 4, Rex 4, Satin Angora 4, Silver Marten 4, Harlequin 4, Satin 6, Champagne d'Argent 6, American Chinchilla 6, Giant Angora 6, New Zealand 6, Cinnamon 6, Creme d'Argent 6, Palomino 6, Blanc de Hotot 6, Silver Fox 6.
- Compact: Standard Chinchilla 4, Mini Satin 4, Jersey Wooly 4, Silver 4, Mini Lop 4, Holland Lop 4, Polish 4, Havana 4, Dutch 4, Mini Rex 4, American Fuzzy Lop 4, Dwarf Hotot 4, Lilac 4, Florida White 4, Netherland Dwarf 4, English Angora 4.

- Full-arch: Checkered Giant 6, Rhinelander 4, English Spot 4, Belgian Hare 4, Tan 4, Britannia Petite 4.
- Cylindrical: Himalayan 4, Czech Frosty 4, Blue Hollister 4.

## Commercial Breeds

- Satin: commercial, satin fur, 6-class; bucks 8.5-10.5 lb, does 9-11 lb. Varieties: Black, Blue, Californian, Chinchilla, Chocolate, Copper, Otter, Red, Siamese, White. Broken is a group. Fine hair shafts and a transparent hair shell create the sheen; body depth approximates width.
- French Lop: commercial, rollback fur, 6-class; bucks 11 lb+, does 11.5 lb+. Groups: Agouti, Broken, Self, Shaded, Ticked, Wide Band. Silvering and Harlequin/Rhinelander patterns are not accepted. Massive, thick-set body; ears rise from the crown, extend below the chin, and lop vertically toward the head.
- French Angora: commercial, wool fur, 4-class; 7.5-10.5 lb. Groups: Agouti, Broken, Pointed White, Self, Shaded, Ticked, Wide Band. No silvered varieties; Broken varieties are accepted. Oval head, erect ears with optional wool on tips, deep rounded body, and wool-covered straight tail.
- Satin Angora: commercial, wool fur, 4-class; 6.5-9.5 lb. Groups: Agouti, Broken, Pointed White, Self, Shaded, Ticked, Wide Band. Oval head, erect ears with plain or slightly tufted tips.
- Silver Marten: commercial, flyback fur, 4-class; bucks 6.5-9 lb, does 7-9.5 lb. Varieties: Black, Blue, Chocolate, Sable. Markings include eye circles, silver-tipped side hairs, nape triangle and collar, silver-white inner ears and nostrils, and a silver-white X.
- American Sable: commercial, rollback fur, 4-class. Rich sepia brown on ears, face, back, legs, and upper tail; saddle fades paler.
- Rex: commercial, rex fur, 4-class; 7.5-9.5 lb. Broad head, erect moderately thick ears, and well-rounded hips with filled loin, rib, and shoulders.
- Harlequin: commercial, flyback fur, 4-class. Japanese is golden fawn or orange with striped second color; Magpie is white with striped second color.
- Champagne d'Argent: commercial, flyback fur, 6-class; 9-12 lb. Bluish white coat with evenly distributed jet-black hairs and dark slate-blue undercolor.
- American Chinchilla: commercial, 6-class; 9-12 lb. Dark slate-blue base, light pearl middle, narrow black top band; narrow jet-black ear lacing.
- Giant Angora: commercial, wool fur, 6-class; bucks 9.5 lb+, does 10 lb+. Ruby-Eyed White with ruby-red eyes and close V-shaped ears.
- New Zealand: commercial, flyback fur, 6-class; bucks 9-11 lb, does 10-12 lb. Black, Blue, Red, and White self varieties plus Broken.
- Cinnamon: commercial, 6-class; 8.5-11 lb. Rust/cinnamon coat with smoke-gray ticking; rust lap spots, butterfly marking, and dark eye circles.
- Creme d'Argent: commercial, silver-ticked cream coat; medium body with rounded back and strong straight legs.
- Californian: commercial, flyback fur, 6-class; 9.5-12 lb. White body with black or chocolate points.
- Argente Brun: commercial, flyback fur, 6-class; 8-9.5 lb. Evenly silvered and frosted chocolate brown.
- Palomino: commercial, flyback fur, 6-class; bucks 8-10 lb, does 9-11 lb. Golden and Lynx varieties.
- Blanc de Hotot: commercial, rollback fur, 6-class; 8-11 lb. Frosty white coat with dark eye bands.
- Silver Fox: commercial, standing fur, 6-class; bucks 9-11 lb, does 10-12 lb. Jet black coat, dark slate undercoat, and evenly interspersed white-tipped hairs.

## Compact Breeds

- Lionhead: compact, wool and rollback fur, 4-class; max 3.75 lb. Listed varieties: Seal, Siamese Sable, Tortoise.
- Standard Chinchilla: compact, 4-class; dark slate-blue base, pearl middle, narrow black top band, and black ear lacing.
- Mini Satin: compact, satin fur, 4-class; max 4.75 lb. Varieties: Black, Blue, Broken, Chinchilla, Chocolate, Chocolate Agouti, Copper, Himalayan, Opal, Otter, Red, Siamese, Silver Marten, Squirrel, Tortoise, White.
- Thrianta: compact, rollback fur, 4-class; 4-6 lb.
- Lilac: compact, rollback fur, 4-class; bucks 5.5-7.5 lb, does 6-8 lb. Medium dove gray with pink tint and blue-gray eyes.
- Florida White: compact, flyback fur, 4-class; 4-6 lb. Pure white with pink eyes.
- Netherland Dwarf: compact, rollback fur, 4-class; max 2.5 lb. Groups: Self, Shaded, Agouti, Tan, AOV.
- English Angora: compact, wool fur, 4-class; 5-7.5 lb. Groups: Agouti, Pointed White, Self, Shaded, Ticked, Wide Band.
- Jersey Wooly: compact, wool fur, 4-class; max 3.5 lb. Groups: AOV, Broken, Self, Shaded, Tan Pattern.
- Silver: compact, flyback fur, 4-class; 4-7 lb. Varieties: Black, Brown, Fawn.
- Polish: compact, 4-class. Varieties: Black, Blue, Broken, Chocolate, Lilac, Blue-Eyed White, Ruby-Eyed White.
- Havana: compact, flyback fur, 4-class; max 6.5 lb. Varieties: Black, Blue, Broken, Chocolate, Lilac.
- Dutch: compact, flyback fur, 4-class; 3.5-5.5 lb. White front half, colored rear half, straight division, and blaze from nose to ears.
- American Fuzzy Lop: compact, wool fur, 4-class; max 4 lb. Groups: Agouti, Broken, Pointed White, Self, Shaded, Tan Pattern, Wide Band.
- Dwarf Hotot: compact, rollback fur, 4-class; max 3 lb. Pure white with dark eye bands.
- Mini Rex: compact, rex fur, 4-class; bucks 3-4.25 lb, does 3.25-4.5 lb. Groups: Self, Shaded, Agouti, Tan, AOV, Broken.
- Mini Lop: compact, rollback fur, 4-class; 4.5-6.5 lb. Groups: Agouti, Broken, Pointed White, Self, Shaded, Ticked, Wide Band.
- Holland Lop: compact, rollback fur, 4-class; max 4 lb. Groups: Agouti, Broken, Pointed White, Self, Shaded, Tan Pattern, Ticked, Wide Band.
- Dwarf Papillon: Chocolate variety, compact, flyback fur, 4-class; 2.5-4.25 lb.

## Cylindrical Breeds

- Himalayan: cylindrical, flyback fur, 4-class; 2.5-4.5 lb. Varieties: Black, Blue, Chocolate, Lilac. Albino with colored points.
- Czech Frosty: cylindrical, slow rollback fur, 4-class; 6.25-8.25 lb. Frosty variety with sepia-tipped guard hairs producing a dark haze.
- Blue Hollister: cylindrical, slow rollback fur, 4-class; 5.5-7.25 lb. Blue only.

## Semi-arch Breeds

- Flemish Giant: semi-arch, rollback fur, 6-class; bucks 13 lb+, does 14 lb+. Varieties: Black, Blue, Fawn, Light Gray, Sandy, Steel Gray, White.
- Giant Chinchilla: semi-arch, flyback fur, 6-class; bucks 12-15 lb, does 13-16 lb. Chinchilla coat banding.
- American: semi-arch, flyback fur, 6-class; 9-12 lb. Blue and White varieties.
- English Lop: semi-arch, flyback fur, 6-class; bucks 10 lb+, does 10.5 lb+. Groups: Agouti, Broken, Self, Shaded, Ticked, Wide Band. Ears at least 21 inches tip to tip.
- Beveren: semi-arch, rollback fur, 6-class. Black, Blue, and White varieties; V-shaped ears.

## Full-arch Breeds

- Checkered Giant: full-arch, flyback fur, 6-class; buck minimum 11 lb, doe minimum 12 lb. Black and Blue varieties; dorsal stripe, butterfly marking, cheek spots, eye circles, and two medium spots on each side.
- Belgian Hare: full-arch, flyback fur, 4-class; 6-9.5 lb. Rich deep red or tan/chestnut coat with slate-blue undercolor; carries body high.
- English Spot: full-arch, flyback fur, 4-class; 5-8 lb. Black, Blue, Chocolate, Gold, Gray, Lilac, Tortoise; nose spots, eye circles, flank spots, and dorsal stripe.
- Rhinelander: full-arch, flyback fur, 4-class. Black (black and orange) and Blue (blue and fawn) varieties; both colors equally represented; 8-10 hip spots per side; extra body spots are a disqualification.
- Tan: full-arch, flyback fur, 4-class; 4.5-5 lb. Black, Blue, Chocolate, Lilac; even tan eye circles and rich tan inner ear lacing.
- Britannia Petite: full-arch, flyback fur, 4-class; max 2.5 lb. Black, Black Otter, Blue-Eyed White, Broken, Chestnut Agouti, Ruby-Eyed White, Sable Marten, Siamese Sable.

## Detailed Breed Notes

### Commercial

- Satin Angora: oval head close to body; ears are straight, erect, and set on top of the head, with plain or slightly tufted tips. The medium-length body has good width and depth with smooth, filled hindquarters; legs are medium boned.
- Silver Marten: medium-length head close to body; large bright, bold eyes; erect ears proportioned to the body. The medium-length body is deep and plump with a gradual curve to the topline; legs are medium boned and straight.
- American Sable: well-shaped, moderately full head; bright, bold eyes; erect ears. Body is medium length, deep, and wide with a smooth continuous curve to the topline; feet and legs are medium in bone and length.
- Rex: broad head close to shoulders; bright, bold eyes; moderately thick erect ears. Body is medium length with rounded hips and well-filled loin, rib, and shoulders; feet and legs are medium boned, straight, and rather short.
- Harlequin: head, ears, and body are well proportioned and gracefully carried; ears are carried in a V shape. Japanese varieties are golden fawn or orange with stripes of a second color; Magpie varieties are white with stripes of a second color. Both have brindled stripes.
- Champagne d'Argent: ears are well furred and erect. Body is medium length with developed shoulders and hindquarters and good depth; feet and legs are medium-fine boned and straight.
- American Chinchilla: medium full-faced head with a well-filled jaw on a short neck; large bright, bold eyes and close-set erect ears with narrow jet-black lacing. Body is medium length with a curved topline; legs are medium boned and straight.
- Giant Angora: oval head close to body and ruby-red, bright, bold eyes. Ears are proportioned to head and body, set on top of the head, and carried in a close V. The body tapers slightly from hindquarters to shoulders and is balanced; legs are medium-heavy boned and the tail is straight and wool covered.
- New Zealand: full head close to shoulders; dark brown, brown, or pink eyes; medium-thick ears rounded at the tip, set high, and erect. Body is medium length and rounded with a gradual curve to the topline; legs are medium-heavy, straight, and medium length.
- Cinnamon: medium-length, medium-width head on a short neck; bright, clear, alert eyes; ears with a strong base and rounded tips. Body is medium length with developed shoulders and hindquarters; feet and legs are medium boned.
- Creme d'Argent: medium-length head close to body; bright, bold, alert eyes; ears with a strong base and rounded tips. Body is medium length with developed shoulders and hindquarters and a rounded back; legs are medium boned, strong, and straight.
- Californian: well-shaped, fairly full head on a short neck close to body; bright, bold pink eyes; erect ears with a strong base. Body is medium length, plump, and firm with a curved topline; legs are medium boned and rather short.
- Argente Brun: body depth approximately equals width. The broad, bold head is set into the shoulders on a short neck; ears are medium length, well furred, close together, and erect; legs are medium boned and straight.
- Palomino: medium-length head close to shoulders; well-furred ears carried erect together. Body is medium size and rounded with a smooth, gradual topline arc; legs are medium boned, straight, and medium length.
- Blanc de Hotot: strong, broad head; dark brown, bold eyes; well-furred V-shaped ears. Body is rounded and thick set with balanced muscle; front legs are medium length and straight, while hind legs are strong and bold.
- Silver Fox: well-formed, full head close to body on a compact neck; clear, bold, bright brown eyes; ears heavily cartilaged at the base and upright. Body is medium length with an arched topline and filled shoulders, rib section, and hindquarters; foot pads are well covered.

### Compact

- Lionhead: shoulders and chest are broad and well filled, matching hindquarter width. The topline blends from strong shoulders through a short midsection over smooth hips and full hindquarters. Legs are medium length and bone for the rabbit's size.
- Standard Chinchilla: head is full from ear base to muzzle, with a well-filled face and jaw; eyes are bright and bold. Erect ears match body color and have black lacing. Feet and legs are medium-fine boned and straight.
- Mini Satin: short, close-coupled body with developed shoulders and hindquarters. The topline rises gradually from ear base to center of hips and falls smoothly to tail base. Head is round and full, more developed in bucks; ears are erect, stocky, and well furred.
- Thrianta: compact, balanced body with shoulders slightly narrower than hips; short, full head with filled cheeks. Ears are erect, stocky, and well furred; legs are short, straight, and medium boned; toenails are dark.
- Lilac: short, well-filled but not coarse head; large, clear, bright blue-gray eyes; moderately short, well-rounded ears. Body has a gradually curving topline; legs are medium boned, short, and straight.
- Florida White: round, full head close to body; stocky, well-furred erect ears; short, close-coupled body with a gradual topline curve.
- Netherland Dwarf: large, round head set high and close to shoulders; round, bold, bright eyes; short, pointed erect ears with a strong base; compact, short body and straight well-furred tail.
- English Angora: short, broad head and bold, bright eyes. Short ears are abundantly fringed and tasseled with wool. The body is short coupled and compact, with a full chest and round shoulders; legs are medium to fine boned.
- Jersey Wooly: bold, rounded head close to shoulders with a short, dense wool cap; bold, bright eyes; short, well-furred erect ears. Body is short and compact with a gradual topline curve; legs are sturdy and straight.
- Silver: head is medium sized and proportioned to body, well set on shoulders and carried erect. Ears have a strong base and are upright; feet and legs are medium in bone and length.
- Mini Rex: full head close to shoulders; thick, relatively short, close-set erect ears; bright, bold eyes. Body is compact, smooth, rather short, and developed; legs are medium to fine boned, straight, and rather short.
- Mini Lop: strong, sturdy head close to shoulders. Rounded, well-furred ears sit on top of head, rise from a strong basal ridge, and lop vertically toward head. Thick, short, straight legs; the body should look rounded and symmetrical from top, side, and back.
- Holland Lop: massive, wide, rounded head high and close to shoulders. Thick, wide, rounded, well-furred ears lop vertically toward head. The crown is strongly defined by cartilage and dense fur; body is short, massive, muscled, compact, and thick set.
- Dwarf Papillon: bold head proportioned to the body and close to it. Stocky body with equal-width shoulders and hindquarters, with a short rounded topline over hindquarters. Ears are proportioned, rounded, erect, substantial, and well furred.
- Polish: medium, full, short head with full cheeks and muzzle; large, bold, expressive, bright eyes. Small well-formed ears are close together and touch all the way up. Body is small, compact, close coupled, and gradually curved; legs are fine boned and short.
- Havana: medium, short, broad head with filled cheeks; medium-sized eyes; rather short ears with a firm base, erect and close together. Body is short and compact with a smooth, continuously curved topline; legs are straight, medium-fine boned, and short.
- Dutch: round, full head with a short neck; bright, bold eyes; stocky, well-furred erect ears. The colored head and ears have a white blaze from nose to ears.
- American Fuzzy Lop: rounded, massive head high and close to shoulders. Ears are full, wide, set on top of head, and lop vertically toward head. Body is short, close coupled, heavily muscled, compact, and balanced; legs are short, thick, and straight.
- Dwarf Hotot: bold head with width between eyes and no visible neck; bold dark-brown eyes; short, substantial, well-furred ears. Body is short, compact, rounded, and gradually curved; legs are short.

### Semi-arch and Cylindrical

- Himalayan: long, neat head; pink, bold, bright eyes; long, narrow cylindrical body with straight topline. Fur is short, fine, and silky; colored points are on ears, nose, tail, and feet.
- Czech Frosty: stocky cylindrical body with a flat topline from nape; shoulders match hindquarter width. Short full head with filled cheeks, especially in bucks; thick, well-furred ears with broad base and open rounded tip; short, straight, medium-boned legs.
- Flemish Giant: large, broad head and reposeful eyes; erect ears with heavy base. Body is long and powerful, with broad full forequarters, hindquarters, and chest; legs are long, large, straight, strong, and powerful.
- Giant Chinchilla: large, shapely head set firmly on shoulders; bold eyes with black lacing. Body is moderately long, massive, and powerful with broad full forequarters and hindquarters; legs are strong, straight, medium length, and fairly heavily boned.
- American: well-shaped narrow head; bright, bold eyes; ears proportionate to the body and tapering to a slight point. The mandolin body has an arched topline; legs are medium boned and straight.
- English Lop: wide, well-shaped head with full cheeks and a slightly wide muzzle. Ears are carried low without a noticeable crown. Body has developed shoulders, midsection, and hindquarters; legs are medium-heavy boned, medium length, straight, and parallel to body.
- Beveren: distinctively curved head with full face and jaw. Body is medium length with broad, meaty depth and a firm loin; legs are medium boned.

### Full-arch

- Checkered Giant: solid colored ears, butterfly marking over nose and muzzle, a solid cheek spot on each side, eye circles, dorsal stripe from ears to tail, and two medium spots on each side.
- Belgian Hare: long, narrow head on a slender neck; large, bold, alert eyes; thin, fine erect ears. Long, narrow body carried high on long, fine legs; long, straight tail in line with backbone.
- English Spot: body is well up off the table with a full arch.
- Rhinelander: body is well arched off the ground with a smooth topline. Dorsal stripe runs from nape to tail; single cheek spot on each side; butterfly, eye circles, ears, and spine show both colors. Black-and-orange has brown eyes; blue-and-fawn has blue or purple eyes.
- Tan: ears are upright, well furred, and laced inside with rich tan; topline is gracefully and moderately arched.
- Britannia Petite: wedge-shaped head broad across forehead and between eyes. Erect, well-furred ears are close together and touch to top. Body is fine boned, diminutive, slender, well off ground, with tucked belly and smooth curved topline; forelegs are fine boned.

## Anatomy and Judging Glossary

- Carriage: how a rabbit carries itself, its pose, and how it carries its ears.
- Dewlap: loose pendulous throat skin, common in does; must be proportionate and is a disqualification in some breeds.
- Tassels: longer wool on top of ears in some wool breeds.
- Cheeks: face below the eyes; for Dutch, rounded colored markings that form the blaze and carry along the jawline.
- Saddle: upper back; for Dutch, where white fur ends and colored fur begins on the upper body.
- Bell ears: ears with large, heavy tips and a distinctive fold.
- Flank: side between rib and hip above the belly.
- Hind leg: foot, hock, stifle, and hip joint. Hip: joint attaching the hind leg to the trunk.
- Arch: gentle spine curvature from neck or shoulders to rear, best viewed in profile.
- Topline: side profile of shoulders, back, and hindquarters.
- Rump: upper rounded hindquarters. Ribs: curved sides behind and under shoulders.
- Shoulder: neck through sixth rib, including upper foreleg joint. Loin: back from last rib to hip joint.
- Forequarter: neck through last rib. Midsection: sixth rib to rear legs. Hindquarter: last rib rearward, including loin, hips, hind legs, and rump.
- Back: top of shoulders, loins, and hindquarters. Neck: connects head and body; for Dutch, a wedge-shaped collar marking behind ears. Muzzle: lower face and nose.
- Hock: weight-bearing foot area. Pastern: ankle and portion below it; rear pastern is below the hock. Stifle: knee joint.
- Crown: strong cartilage ridge at the top of the head forming the ear base on some lop breeds.
- Dewclaws: on the inside of front legs only; none on hind legs.

## Additional Rabbit Terms

- Fault: a deviation from perfection or physical imperfection.
- Buck: an intact male rabbit. Doe: an intact female rabbit.
- Tattoo: a permanent identification mark in the left ear or both ears; a right-ear tattoo indicates an ARBA-registered rabbit.
- Lactate: to produce milk. Kindle: the act of giving birth by rabbits.
- Pedigree: genealogy of male and female ancestors that includes birth date, parents, grandparents, great-grandparents, and may include color, weight, and registration numbers.
- Fryer or single fryer: market rabbit under 10 weeks old, weighing 3.5-5.5 lb. Roaster: under 6 months old, 5.5-9 lb. Stewer: over 6 months old and over 8 lb.
- White toenail: a nail without pigmentation, showing only the pink cast of the blood vessel.
- Breeder: a person raising a breed or variety to the Standard of Perfection, or a rabbit used primarily to produce offspring.
- Breed: a class of domestic rabbits that reproduces distinctive characteristics such as fur, markings, shape, and size. Purebred: loosely, a rabbit meeting breed requirements for at least three generations.
- Mutation: a sudden physical change caused by a gene alteration; Rex and Satin are well-known fur-structure mutations.
- Definition: sharpness and clarity of a color break on a hair shaft, or contrast between two or more colors. Prime: ideal flesh and coat condition. Adult: a mature rabbit of breeding age.
- Mandolin: a semi-arch body whose arch begins at the back of the shoulders rather than the nape of the neck.
- Intermediate: in eligible 6-class breeds with a minimum ideal senior weight of 9 lb or more, a rabbit 6 months to under 8 months old, subject to breed-standard weights.
- Senior: 6 months or older in 4-class breeds; 8 months or older in 6-class breeds, subject to minimum senior weight where stated.
- Junior: under 6 months old. Pre-junior: under 3 months old, shown separately by sex and variety only where a breed's showroom classes allow it.
- Strain: a genetically related bloodline with distinguishable heritable characteristics such as type, color, or coat.
